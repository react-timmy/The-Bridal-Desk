# The Bridal Desk Website PRD

## 1. Product Summary

The Bridal Desk is a founder-led bridal operations brand for Nigerian brides who want a calm, coordinated, and professionally managed wedding experience.

The first website should not present The Bridal Desk as a generic wedding planning service. It should position the brand as:

> The operating system behind stress-free weddings.

The business starts with human-led wedding coordination, then expands into a larger bridal infrastructure company: vendor networks, concierge support, sponsor partnerships, verified vendors, training, and eventually wedding management software.

The website's job is to convert brides into consultation leads while making the brand feel premium, operationally serious, and scalable.

## 2. Goals

### Primary Goal

Drive qualified brides to book a consultation with The Bridal Desk.

### Secondary Goals

- Establish The Bridal Desk as a premium bridal operations brand, not only a day-of coordination service.
- Communicate the signature operating model: the Bridal Command Center.
- Build trust around Desire Cole as Founder and Lead Coordinator.
- Create a clear path for potential sponsors, partners, and vendors to express interest.
- Prepare the brand for future expansion into vendor verification, bridal events, coordinator training, and bridal software.

## 3. Target Audience

### Primary Audience

Brides aged roughly 22-35 planning weddings in Lagos, Southwest Nigeria, and other Nigerian urban markets.

They may be:

- Working professionals with limited time.
- Brides managing large family expectations.
- Brides coordinating many vendors at once.
- Brides planning traditional, white wedding, or multi-day wedding events.
- Brides who want a polished wedding experience without personally managing every detail.

### Secondary Audience

- Wedding vendors who want structured partnerships.
- Beauty, fashion, hospitality, lifestyle, and bridal brands interested in founding partnerships or sponsorships.
- Future coordinators who may join or train under The Bridal Desk.

## 4. Core Problem

Weddings are operationally complex, especially in Nigeria where guest counts can be large, family involvement is high, and vendor coordination is often fragmented.

The bride is often expected to manage:

- Makeup artists.
- Hair stylists.
- Photographers and videographers.
- Caterers.
- Decorators.
- Dress changes.
- Transportation.
- Bridesmaids.
- Family members.
- Vendor timelines.
- Payments.
- Last-minute emergencies.

The bride should not have to be the project manager of her own wedding.

## 5. Positioning

### Brand Position

The Bridal Desk is a bridal operations company that helps brides move from wedding chaos to calm execution.

### Founder Position

Desire Cole should be presented as:

**Founder + Lead Coordinator**

The site should make it clear that the business is currently founder-led, personal, and hands-on, while also showing that The Bridal Desk is being built as a scalable company with systems, trained support, vendor relationships, and future technology.

Suggested copy direction:

> The Bridal Desk is currently founder-led, which allows every bride to receive personal, hands-on coordination. As we grow, we are building a trusted network of coordinators, vendors, and systems that make stress-free weddings repeatable.

### What To Avoid

- Do not say "I am just starting out."
- Do not overstate audience size, client volume, or sponsor reach.
- Do not make the brand sound like a large agency if it is currently solo-led.
- Do not lead with "wedding planning" as the only offer.
- Do not make public pricing the focus.

## 6. Signature Concept: Bridal Command Center

The website should anchor The Bridal Desk around the **Bridal Command Center**.

This is the operational layer behind a wedding. It is not necessarily a literal software dashboard in v1. It is the branded system The Bridal Desk uses to keep the wedding moving.

### Bridal Command Center Modules

1. **Timeline Desk**
   - Wedding morning schedule.
   - Ceremony and reception flow.
   - Vendor arrival windows.
   - Dress change timing.
   - Photo and video moments.

2. **Vendor Desk**
   - Vendor contact tracking.
   - Arrival confirmation.
   - Setup monitoring.
   - Issue escalation.
   - Day-of coordination.

3. **Bride Concierge**
   - Direct support for the bride.
   - Calm communication.
   - Personal reminders.
   - Beauty, outfit, transport, and comfort checks.

4. **People Desk**
   - Bridesmaids.
   - Family members.
   - Key contacts.
   - Processional and reception coordination.

5. **Emergency Desk**
   - Bridal emergency kit planning.
   - Backup contacts.
   - Last-minute issue handling.
   - Fast escalation for vendor or timing problems.

6. **Partner Desk**
   - Sponsor and vendor collaboration.
   - Bridal gift boxes.
   - Brand placements.
   - Launch events and bridal education activations.

## 8. User Journeys

### Bride Journey

1. Bride lands on the homepage.
2. She immediately understands that The Bridal Desk removes wedding-day stress.
3. She sees the Bridal Command Center as a structured coordination system.
4. She reviews services.
5. She understands that pricing is consultation-led.
6. She submits an inquiry.
7. The Bridal Desk follows up to schedule a consultation.

### Sponsor / Partner Journey

1. Brand owner or marketing contact lands on the site.
2. They see The Bridal Desk's audience and bridal positioning.
3. They understand the difference between sponsorship, partnership, and vendor relationships.
4. They submit a partner inquiry.

### Future Coordinator Journey

1. Future coordinator learns that The Bridal Desk is building a scalable coordination network.
2. They understand training/certification may come later.
3. They can register interest only if this becomes part of a later release.

## 9. Feature Requirements

### Hero Section

The hero must communicate:

- Brand name: The Bridal Desk.
- Core promise: stress-free wedding operations.
- Market context: Nigerian brides or Lagos weddings.
- Primary CTA: Book a Consultation.
- Secondary CTA: Explore the Command Center.

Suggested headline direction:

> The Bridal Desk

Suggested support copy:

> Bridal coordination and wedding-day operations for brides who want calm, clarity, and a beautifully managed celebration.

### Problem Section

Show the operational complexity of weddings through concise, visual content.

Use copy such as:

> Your wedding should not depend on you answering 47 calls while getting your makeup done.

Include chaos categories:

- Vendors
- Family
- Bridesmaids
- Transport
- Timelines
- Payments
- Emergencies

### Bridal Command Center Section

This should be the strongest differentiator on the website.

It should feel like an elegant operations system, not a generic feature list.

Required content:

- What the Bridal Command Center is.
- What it manages.
- Why it reduces stress.
- How it supports the bride before and during the wedding.

### Services Section

Services should be grouped as premium operational offers:

1. **Day-of Coordination**
   - For brides who have planned the wedding but need professional execution.

2. **Full Wedding Planning**
   - For brides who want deeper planning support from concept to wedding day.

3. **Bridal Concierge**
   - For personal support throughout the bridal journey.

4. **Vendor Coordination**
   - For managing communication, timelines, and expectations across vendors.

5. **Emergency Support**
   - For wedding-day issue handling, emergency kits, and backup planning.

Pricing should not be displayed publicly.

Use consultation-led wording:

> Every wedding is different. We begin with a consultation to understand your date, location, guest count, vendors, and support needs.

### The Bridal Room Section

The Bridal Room should be presented as a launch/community concept:

> A bridal preparation masterclass and experience for brides who want practical guidance before the big day.

Potential topics:

- Wedding budgeting.
- Vendor selection.
- Wedding morning timeline.
- Bridal emergency planning.
- Food safety for catering.
- Mental preparation before the wedding.

This section may also support sponsor interest.

### Partner Opportunities Section

This section should explain three relationship types:

1. **Sponsorship**
   - A brand provides cash, product, service, or media support in exchange for visibility and commercial benefits.

2. **Partnership**
   - Two businesses collaborate because both benefit.

3. **Vendor Relationship**
   - The Bridal Desk recommends or works with a vendor to serve brides.

The section should invite brands to become founding partners, especially:

- Beauty brands.
- Fashion brands.
- Bridal designers.
- Hotels.
- Event centres.
- Photographers.
- Makeup artists.
- Cake vendors.
- Printing companies.
- Gift brands.
- Skincare and hair brands.

### About Section

The About section should center Desire Cole as the founder while keeping The Bridal Desk as the brand.

Required points:

- Desire is the Founder and Lead Coordinator.
- The business is founder-led at the beginning.
- The brand uses trusted vendors, assistants, and partners as needed.
- The long-term vision is a bridal operations company, not a one-woman ceiling.

## 10. Design Requirements

### Visual Direction

The site should feel like a premium streaming-platform-inspired bridal operations experience, similar in confidence and polish to Netflix or Prime Video interfaces, but adapted for bridal services.

Use:

- Cinematic hero imagery.
- Large editorial panels.
- Horizontal rails for services, modules, or stories.
- Deep contrast between warm ivory and burgundy.
- Dusty rose surfaces and accents.
- Subtle gold highlights for premium cues.
- Rich photography or generated bridal visuals.
- Strong section transitions.

Avoid:

- Generic wedding planner templates.
- Overly cute bridal graphics.
- Excessive floral decoration.
- Flat brochure-style service grids.
- A one-note pink palette.
- Public package prices.

### Color Palette

Primary palette:

- Blush / Dusty Rose: `#C98F9B`
- Warm Ivory: `#FFF8EF`
- Deep Burgundy: `#5A1022`
- Subtle Gold: `#C6A45C`

Supporting neutrals:

- Soft Charcoal: `#24181B`
- Muted Mauve: `#8E5964`
- Pale Rose: `#F3DDE1`
- Champagne: `#EFE2C5`

### UX Requirements

- The first viewport must make the brand name and core promise obvious.
- The main CTA must remain easy to access.
- The inquiry form should feel premium and simple, not like a long administrative form.
- Mobile design must be polished, with no overlapping text or cramped buttons.
- Forms should use clear labels and helpful placeholder text.
- Use high-quality imagery that directly signals bridal preparation, coordination, elegance, and Nigerian wedding context where possible.

## 11. Content Strategy

### Tone

The voice should be calm, premium, direct, and operationally confident.

The copy should make the bride feel:

- Relieved.
- Understood.
- Protected.
- Professionally supported.
- Like her wedding has a system behind it.

### Messaging Pillars

1. **Calm**
   - The bride should not carry the stress.

2. **Control**
   - Every moving part has a place.

3. **Trust**
   - Vendors, timelines, and people are coordinated.

4. **Experience**
   - The wedding should feel beautiful and well-run.

5. **Scale**
   - The Bridal Desk is building infrastructure for weddings, not only offering one-off coordination.

## 12. Business Rules

- Do not show public prices in v1.
- Do not accept online payments in v1.
- Do not require user accounts in v1.
- Do not launch a vendor marketplace in v1.
- Do not imply sponsors are confirmed unless they are real.
- Do not imply The Bridal Desk has employees if it is currently founder-led.
- Use consultation-led quoting for bride services.
- Use partner inquiry for sponsors, vendors, and collaborators.

## 14. Roadmap

### Phase 1: Manual Service Launch

- Launch website.
- Capture bride leads.
- Book consultations.
- Coordinate 5-10 weddings or bridal projects manually.
- Document every operational problem.
- Build a small vendor contact base.

### Phase 2: Repeatable Operations

- Standardize checklists.
- Create coordination templates.
- Build Bridal Command Center processes.
- Develop bridal emergency kit offer.
- Launch The Bridal Room masterclass.
- Start founding partner conversations.

### Phase 3: Network Growth

- Build vendor verification standards.
- Create preferred vendor directory.
- Train assistant coordinators.
- Package sponsor opportunities with real audience data.
- Expand beyond founder-only execution.

### Phase 4: Technology Layer

- Build internal wedding management tools only after manual demand is proven.
- Potential future features:
  - Budget tracking.
  - Vendor tracking.
  - Guest management.
  - Timeline builder.
  - Payments visibility.
  - Bride reminders.
  - Vendor ratings and reviews.

## 15. Acceptance Criteria

The PRD is complete when it clearly enables a designer or developer to build a v1 website that:

- Presents The Bridal Desk as a bridal operations company.
- Prioritizes bride consultation leads.
- Uses Bridal Command Center as the main differentiator.
- Avoids public pricing.
- Includes sponsor and partner pathways without making them the main focus.
- Uses the blush, ivory, burgundy, and gold palette.
- Defines required forms, sections, messaging, and future roadmap.
- Avoids building a full software app before manual service validation.
